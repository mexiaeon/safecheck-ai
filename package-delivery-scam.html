Package Delivery Text Scams: Warning Signs and What to Do

Learn how fake delivery texts pressure people to pay fees or share information. See warning signs, safer ways to check a delivery, and what to do if you clicked.

Fake package-delivery messages often claim a parcel is delayed, held, or cannot be delivered until you pay a small fee or update your address. The message may imitate a postal service or familiar carrier.

Common warning signs

  • An unexpected message with no order details you can independently verify.
  • A redelivery, customs, or address-correction fee requested through a text link.
  • A shortened link, misspelled company name, or domain that does not match the carrier.
  • A threat that the parcel will be returned unless you act immediately.
  • A request for card details, passwords, or a one-time code.

Check a delivery safely

Do not use the message link. Open the carrier's official app or type its known website address yourself. Enter a tracking number from a real order there. Do not pay a fee just to discover what an unexpected message is about.

If you clicked or paid

Do not enter more information. If you entered card details, contact the card issuer using the number on your card. Change any exposed password on the genuine service and enable multi-factor authentication. Keep the message and payment records.

Official guidance

See the U.S. Postal Inspection Service package-delivery smishing guidance and FTC scam resources.
