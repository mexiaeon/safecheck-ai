Phishing Text Messages: Signs a Text Is Trying to Steal Your Information

Recognize phishing texts that impersonate banks, delivery companies, government agencies, and online accounts. Learn how to verify a message without using its link.

Phishing texts (sometimes called smishing) try to persuade you to reveal information or visit a fake website. They may pretend to come from a bank, delivery company, government agency, or online service.

Five signs a text may be phishing

  • Urgency: it threatens account closure, a fine, or lost access unless you act now.
  • Secret information: it asks for a password, PIN, or one-time security code.
  • Unexpected link: it sends you to a sign-in or payment page you did not request.
  • Unusual payment: it asks for gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers, or another hard-to-reverse method.
  • Impersonation: it uses a familiar company name but the sender or web address does not check out.

Verify the sender

Do not reply, tap the link, or call a number included in a suspicious text. Contact the organization through its official app, a website address you type yourself, or a number from a trusted statement or card. A familiar logo or company name does not prove who sent the message.

Report suspicious texts

In the United States, the FTC recommends forwarding spam texts to 7726 (SPAM) and reporting them at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Carrier reporting options vary by country. See FTC scam resources.
