Task Job Scams: Why You Should Never Pay to Unlock Earnings

Task scams promise easy online earnings for likes, ratings, or repetitive tasks, then demand deposits to unlock work or withdraw a balance. Learn the warning signs.

Task scams often begin with an unexpected message offering simple online work, such as rating products, liking content, or completing small tasks. A website or app may show a growing balance to make the opportunity look legitimate.

The deposit trap

A common warning sign appears when a platform says you must deposit your own money to unlock tasks, fix a negative balance, reach a withdrawal threshold, or release earnings. The displayed balance may be fictional. Sending money does not guarantee you can withdraw anything.

Other warning signs

  • An unsolicited job offer through text or a messaging app.
  • Vague work with unusually high promised pay for little effort.
  • A registration fee, training fee, or deposit required before earning.
  • Payment demanded in cryptocurrency or another hard-to-reverse form.
  • A recruiter who avoids verifiable company details or pressures you to act quickly.

Protect yourself

Do not deposit money to receive wages or unlock tasks. Research the company using contact information you find independently. Do not rely on screenshots of earnings or testimonials supplied by the recruiter. Never share bank passwords or one-time codes.

If you already paid

Stop sending money, even if the platform claims one more payment will release your balance. Contact your bank or payment provider promptly through its official channel. Save messages, usernames, wallet addresses, receipts, and website details.

Official guidance

Read the FTC consumer scam guidance and report suspected fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
