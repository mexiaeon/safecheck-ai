Fake Refund Scams: How to Spot a Refund Phishing Message

A refund message can be genuine or a phishing attempt. Learn the difference between a completed refund notice and a message designed to steal account or payment details.

Scammers send messages claiming that you are owed a refund, a payment failed, or money is waiting to be released. They may want you to open a fake sign-in page, disclose payment details, or pay a fee.

Warning signs

  • You must sign in through the message link to receive a refund.
  • The sender asks for a password, one-time code, full card number, or bank login.
  • You must pay an upfront fee to release money supposedly owed to you.
  • The message uses urgency, threats, or an unexpected deadline.
  • The sender address or website domain does not match the real company.

Verify a refund safely

Do not follow an unexpected refund link. Open the retailer's or payment provider's official app, or type its known website address yourself. Check your order history and account activity there. A notice that a refund has already been processed is different from a demand to sign in, pay, or disclose secrets—but verify unexpected notices independently.

If you shared information

Contact your bank or card issuer through a trusted number. Change exposed passwords on the real website, especially if reused elsewhere. If you shared a verification code, review account sessions and security settings immediately.
